Situation importance of baseball ball–strike counts

Related research · This legacy address redirects to the research page.

A strike’s effect on a plate appearance depends on the current ball–strike count. We modeled count transitions as a Markov chain and analyzed situation importance through differences in outcome probabilities after a ball or strike.

  • Method: situation importance from count transitions
  • Analysis: importance and pitch velocity

Problem and context

Beyond examining pitch velocity or outcome in isolation, the research asks how to quantify the count in which a pitch occurs. We aimed to interpret pitcher behavior and outcomes in the context of situation importance.

My role

I participated as co-first author. The public repository includes code for count-transition data, outcome probabilities and importance calculations, and velocity comparisons.

Approach

Counts as states

Using MLB Statcast data, the model combines 12 ball–strike states with terminal plate-appearance outcomes. Estimated next-state probabilities form a discrete-time Markov chain.

Plate-appearance outcome probabilities

Calculates the probability of reaching an out or on-base outcome from each count. Changes in outcome probability after a ball vs. a strike contribute to Importance of Moment.

Importance and pitch characteristics

Public analysis code compares count-specific importance with mean fastball velocity and separately examines all pitchers and an elite group.

Scope

The research examines fastball velocity at high-importance counts and the elite group’s two-strike characteristics. These are observational associations, not evidence that importance causes velocity changes.

Count probabilities depend on the pitch data and state definitions. Applications to player evaluation or game strategy require additional batter, pitcher, and game-context factors.
