About

I understand data meaning and structure and design ML approaches for the problem at hand.

I researched imbalanced learning and worked on connecting real-world data to analysis and model training in industry. These experiences inform my approach to ML, which starts with understanding data meaning and relationships.

From research to practice

Graduate studies Learning suited to data distributions

During my master’s and doctoral studies, I investigated learning bias in imbalanced data. I examined whether difficult minority-class samples should be removed as noise and whether every dataset needs the same amount of augmentation. My focus was on adapting learning to class distributions and overlap, rather than simply equalizing sample counts.

Master’s research · Efficient oversampling · Doctoral research · Preventing bias in both classes

Industry Training and evaluation with real-world data

In industry, I linked and aligned manufacturing data to build analysis and training datasets. I needed to verify whether records referred to the same target and whether their information was available at prediction time. Applying models also required checking record correspondence and the context in which data would be used.

Manufacturing data validation and linkage · Time-series alignment and ML pipelines

A continuing focus Understanding the data itself

Research and industry experience showed me that how we understand data determines where analysis and modeling begin. We need to define what constitutes one target, which state to predict, and how to evaluate the result. I therefore place particular importance on understanding data meaning and relationships, then connecting problem definition with learning methods.

Defining closing and continuing commercial areas · Evaluation criteria for actual use
